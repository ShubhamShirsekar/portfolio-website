## Overview

A product classification model built for the **L'Oréal and KEDGE Hackathon** (Jan 2026, L'Oréal, Paris, France).

**Team:** 5 people. I was the team lead.

---

## The Problem

L'Oréal gave us **6,240 labelled product descriptions**. Each product is tagged with the labels it suits, out of **33 in total**: skin concerns, skin type, sensitivity, age group, gender and routine step (about 4 per product). The brief: run a **sustainable** ML model that recreates those labels from the description text as accurately as possible.

---

## Approach

- **Fine-tuned RoBERTa-large** as a multi-label classifier, with a tuned decision threshold per label
- **Distilled it into DistilRoBERTa** (knowledge distillation): a smaller student model trained to mimic the large teacher's predictions
- **Quantised the student to INT8** for a smaller saved model
- **Tracked emissions with CodeCarbon** for every training run

---

## Results

Before splitting, rare age and gender labels were oversampled, taking the 6,224 cleaned descriptions to 6,745 rows. Scores are on a **20% test split of those rows (1,349)**. Weighted F1 averages the per-label F1 scores, weighted by how often each label appears. Jaccard is the overlap between the predicted and true label sets for each product.

| Model | Weighted F1 | Macro F1 | Jaccard | Parameters | Saved size | Training time | Training emissions |
|-------|-------------|----------|---------|------------|------------|---------------|--------------------|
| **RoBERTa-large** (teacher) | 78.2% | 77.0% | 55.8% | 355M | 1,360 MB | 214 min | 0.0318 kg CO₂ |
| **DistilRoBERTa** (student) | 76.3% | 74.5% | 52.6% | 82M | 190 MB (INT8) | 10.9 min | 0.0011 kg CO₂ |

The student keeps **97.6% of the teacher's weighted F1** (96.8% of its macro F1) with **77% fewer parameters**.

### What the savings compare

- **Training time (−95%) and training emissions (−96.5%)** compare training the student with training the teacher. Distillation needs the teacher trained first, so these figures do **not** include the teacher's own 214 minutes and 0.0318 kg CO₂. Across the whole pipeline, distillation costs more to train than the teacher alone.
- **Emissions** were measured with **CodeCarbon** during each training run.
- **The lasting benefit is at prediction time.** The student is about 4× smaller, and 190 MB once quantised versus 1,360 MB, so every prediction needs much less compute. We didn't benchmark prediction speed during the hackathon, so there's no milliseconds-per-prediction figure here.
